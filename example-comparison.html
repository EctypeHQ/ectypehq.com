How to read this page

Reading the page

A fingerprint
A file that Ectype fits to a table once: a model of each column and of how columns move together, with no rows. This page compares two of them and reads neither table.
A measured property
One thing a fingerprint records about a column or a pair of columns: the shape of a distribution, its location and spread, the share of empty cells, the strength of an association.
First and second
The first fingerprint is the reference (the original); the second is the one compared with it.
Purple bars, teal outline
In every chart the first fingerprint is drawn as purple filled bars and the second as a teal outline, with a legend line under the chart.
Drifted
A measured property of a column, or of a pair of columns, moved by more than sampling noise explains.
Sampling noise and the threshold
Two samples of one population never agree exactly. Each property has a threshold set from the noise of two samples of these sizes; the page says how many times its threshold a property moved.
False-discovery rate
Of the properties called drifted, at most this share is expected to be noise.
Stand-in tokens
A pseudonymised column's labels are replaced independently in each fingerprint, so equal labels mean nothing: the column is compared by its profile (how concentrated it is), not by its labels.

What this page cannot show

  • The rows themselves.
  • The targets the validator holds back (sealed): only how far each moved is used.
  • Whether a change matters for your use of the data.
  • Why a column changed.

What this page does not describe

Everything in the two fingerprints and their comparison that the report reads is described above.
