Privacy

This fingerprint was fitted under the standard policy. Under it:

  • A value seen in fewer than 10 rows is pooled with other rare values.
  • Each number is stored as at most 100 quantiles between its 0.5th and 99.5th percentiles, so its extremes are not in the fingerprint or on this page.
  • Stored numbers are rounded to 6 significant digits.
  • No group of fewer than 10 rows is described on its own.

What this does and does not mean

  • No row of the source table is in the fingerprint or on this page.
  • Values seen in only a few rows are pooled, so none of them is shown.

These measures limit what the fingerprint stores about any one row. They are not a formal privacy guarantee: a table with few rows or many columns can still describe an unusual record closely.

How to read this page

What this page is

This page describes a table from its fingerprint: a file that Ectype fits to the table once. A fingerprint holds a model of each column, a model of how columns move together and the rules found in the table. It holds no rows, and this page was drawn from it without the table.

The engine is the Ectype software that fitted the fingerprint. The policy is the set of limits on what a fingerprint may store, such as how many rows must share a value before the value is kept; see Privacy. A column is modeled when the fingerprint holds a distribution fitted to it, and its chart is drawn from that distribution.

Reading the charts

Empty cells
The share of rows with no value in the column, drawn as a short rose bar.
A histogram bar
The share of rows whose value falls in that stretch of the axis.
A point mass
One exact value held by many rows (often 0), drawn as a rose line with a dot, apart from the bars.
The triangle
Marks the median: half of the values lie below it.
Clipped axis ends
Where the policy stops storing the tails, the axis ends; the true smallest and largest values are not stored.
Log scale
Each step along the axis multiplies the value; used for long-tailed numbers.
Pooled rare values
Values seen in too few rows to be stored one by one, added up into one bar.
N more values
The stored values that are not drawn one by one, added up.
Heaps
Values pile up on round numbers, such as multiples of 5 or 100, more than a smooth distribution would give.
Tied directly to another column
The model draws this column's value given the other column's value, a closer tie than the general model of how columns move together.
Stand-in tokens
Labels that replace the values of a column that looks personal; they keep the frequencies, not the values.
Strength
How closely two columns are tied, from 0 (unrelated) to 1. Rank correlation is used for two numbers or dates, Cramér's V for two categories, the correlation ratio for a number and a category.

How a column is kept

The words at the top right of a column's card.

Stored quantiles, stored values, stored frequencies
The column's distribution is kept as it was measured: evenly spaced percentiles of a number or date, or the share of rows of each value.
Point masses and a curve
A number where a few exact values hold many rows: those values are kept one by one, and a smooth distribution covers the rest.
A named family (normal, gamma, Poisson …)
The column is kept as that distribution's few parameters.
Computed
Not modeled on its own: worked out from other columns by the formula shown.
Modeled as a gap
Not modeled on its own: the distance to another column is modeled, so the two stay in order. The chart draws that distance.
Share of a total, sum of its parts
Columns bound by a sum: the total or its parts are worked out so that they add up.
Regenerated key
A unique identifier. Its values are not kept; generated rows get new ones of the same shape.
Repeating key
An identifier shared by several rows. How many rows share a key is modeled; the key values are not kept.
Constant
Every row holds the same value.
Left out
The fingerprint holds nothing of this column's values.

What this page cannot show

  • The rows themselves.
  • The exact minimum and maximum of a number.
  • Values seen in fewer rows than the policy allows.
  • Any value of a column that was left out of the model.
  • How three or more columns depend on each other jointly: the model holds it, the page states pairs.
  • The distribution of a column computed from others: the page gives its rule instead.
  • The measurements the fingerprint keeps sealed for checking generated data.
  • Whether any single record is correct.
